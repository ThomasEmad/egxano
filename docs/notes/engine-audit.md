# EGXANO Technical Engine Audit — why the reads feel unconvincing

Date: 2026-11-14. Auditor: external review, investigation only (engine untouched).
Scope: `src/lib/technical/**` (tech-signals@4, technical@4, structure@1, zones@1, stance@1) plus the data paths that feed it. Empirical replay: `scripts/invest/sample-engine.ts`, raw cache in `.cache/history/`. All numbers in section E come from that script's printed output.

**TL;DR — five root causes, ranked by expected impact on analysis quality:**

1. **The BUY decision can fire without volume confirmation and at the top of the range, by construction** — the "excellence gate" (`finalizeScore`, signals.ts:163-171) only clamps raw > 80, but the BUY threshold (62 compressed ≈ raw 71, verified numerically) sits *below* the gate. Verified live: the real engine returns `buy` with `relVol 0.390` and `rangePos60 100.0` (score 63). The gate protects the score *display*, not the *decision*.
2. **Triple-counted trend evidence** — trend (35%), momentum (30%) and risk (15%) all score the same underlying fact family (price above rising short/long averages). A tape in a plain uptrend earns ≈3 of 4 dimensions simultaneously from one phenomenon; the "confluence score" is largely one signal counted three times, which is why scores cluster high in uptrends and the label adds little information.
3. **The rich layers are decorative** — structure (BOS/CHoCH, multi-timeframe trend), supply/demand zones, VSA and the analytical-quality score are computed but *never read by the scorer*. `computeStanceFromSnapshot` consumes only EMA facts, RSI/MACD/Stoch, OBV/relVol, ATR and pivot-S/R levels (signals.ts:722-936); VSA and quality are hard-nulled since technical@4 (analyze.ts:578, 588). Zones' output never touches the stance; only `supportResistance()` levels do (analyze.ts:555-559).
4. **The trade plan is mostly mechanical percentages, not structure** — in the "chasing" branch (price > 6% above nearest support, i.e. the majority of BUY cases after a run), stop = `lc × 0.955` (a flat −4.5%), entry = ±0.4% band around the close, target = 2R (trade-plan.ts:166-205). ATR is only used when `atrPct > 4` for chasing or as a buffer when support-anchored. EGX tick size is respected nowhere (no tick logic exists in the repo; all levels rounded to 0.01).
5. **The public data path is thin and close-only** — the only public history endpoint (`/api/v1/market/history`) serves sparse MarketPrice close points (16 rows for COMI across all ranges, including intraday instants), no volume, capped 500 rows. Board stances run on a 300-calendar-day close-only window (≈200 sessions, signals-service.ts:35) where EMA200/cross checks are marginally or never computable. Historical volume — the entire volume dimension — is only available inside the server (MarketPrice rows); a replay of the engine from public data cannot reconstruct it (proven in E: volume-missing cap 70 → max compressed 61 < 62 → BUY structurally unreachable from that corpus).

Sections A–D below are the code-level evidence; section E is the empirical replay.

**Rounding.** `round(x, 4)` (indicators.ts:49-52) is applied *inside* `ema()`, `rsi()`, `macd()`, `atr()` etc., i.e. the rounded value is what the scorer compares against (`lastClose > m.ema20`). At 4 digits this is practically harmless (worst case a tie-break on a 0.00005-gap), but it *is* premature rounding inside a decision path; the correct pattern is round-at-display. The snapshot JSON then rounds *everything* to 2 dp via `roundReplacer` (analyze.ts:41-44) — this one matters more: the LLM narrative and any client recomputation see 2-dp indicator values that can disagree with the 4-dp decision inputs at band boundaries.

**Redundancy map (who duplicates whose evidence):**

- **Uptrend family (one phenomenon, three scorers):** price>EMA20 earns trend 8/30 (signals.ts:451-454) AND risk `extensionPoints` up to 8/15 (L510-514) AND pushes RSI into 45-68 (momentum 6-10/10) AND lifts MACD>0 (10/10) AND rangePos60 upper band AND OBV slope>0 (8/10). Four of four dimensions reward the same underlying move.
- **RSI vs Stochastic vs MACD:** all three are momentum oscillators over ≤26-session windows; the snapshot momentum dim runs all three (max 30 = 10+10+10, signals.ts:765-781). They are near-perfectly co-moving in trends; their disagreement is informative precisely when the score treats them as independent confirmation. The light engine drops Stochastic (max 30 = 10+10+10 from RSI+MACD+rangePos60 — where rangePos is *also* a momentum-over-60-sessions measure).
- **OBV slope vs relVol:** the volume dim (max 20) gives 8 for OBV>0 and up to 10 for relVol bands — but both are driven by the same up-day volume; a single heavy up-day with accumulation maxes 18/20 = 90% of the dimension from one bar.
- **SMA family vs EMA family:** `trendInfo`/`crossType` (SMA) are kept only for tests; no double-scoring there, but ~120 lines of dead production code invite drift.

Math-check verdict: **no future-leak, no Wilder-vs-simple-average bug, EMA seeding correct, MACD signal/histogram correct, Bollinger σ correct (population), Stochastic windows correct with a documented flat-window skip quirk, OBV slope window as documented.** The math is clean; the problems are calibration, redundancy, and what is (not) wired into the decision.

## B) Scoring / decision audit

### Dimensions and weights (signals.ts:98-116)

| Dimension | Weight | Max (light engine) | Max (snapshot engine) | Components |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trend | 35 | 30 | 30 | price>EMA20 (8), price>EMA50 (7), EMA20>EMA50 (8), light: EMA20 slope streak≥3 (7) / snapshot: price>EMA200 (7) |
| momentum | 30 | 30 | 30 | RSI bands (10), MACD line+hist (10), light: rangePos60 (10) / snapshot: Stochastic (10) |
| volume | 20 | 20 | 20 | OBV slope>0 (8), relVol bands (10; +2 unreachable — see below) |
| risk | 15 | 15 | 15 | extension>EMA20 (8: ≤4%→8, ≤8%→5, >8%→0), daily vol (7: ≤3%→7, ≤5%→4, else 0) |

Volume max is effectively 18, not 20: OBV gives 8 (accumulation deliberately "not proof"), relVol bands top out at 10 — reachable only when relVol ∈ (2,3] on an up day. So `allDimsStrong` (≥80% of own max) for the volume dim requires ≥14.4/20 → OBV>0 AND relVol 2-3× up-day. **A normal confirmed up-move (relVol 1.1×) earns 14/20 = 70% → allDimsStrong is false on most genuinely good days** — the gate then caps any raw > 80 at 80 (→ displayed 73). The practical consequence: displayed scores above 73 require an unusually climactic volume day, and *every* raw 71-80 tape displays 73-80 regardless of whether relVol was 0.4× or 1.2× — the score cannot distinguish strong participation from thin participation inside that band (see the verified relVol 0.39 BUY at score 63; raw 72).

### Score finalization (`finalizeScore`, signals.ts:158-172) — verified arithmetic

1. Missing-evidence caps: no volume → raw ≤70 (→ compressed 61); no risk → raw ≤75 (→ 67); both → 70.
2. Excellence gate: raw > 80 requires every *present* dim ≥80% AND relVol ≥ 1.3, else raw := 80 (→ 73).
3. Compression: `final = round(100·(raw/100)^1.4)`; anchors verified: raw 71 → 62, 75 → 67, 80 → 73, 85 → 80, 90 → 86, 100 → 100.

**The decisive structural flaw: BUY_SCORE=62 corresponds to raw ≈ 71.1 — below the gate's 80 trigger.** The gate therefore only shapes the score *display* for already-high raw scores; it never gates the BUY decision itself. A raw-72 tape with volume dim at 55% and relVol 0.39 (verified run, section E) prints 63 → BUY. The design intent "80+ must be rare and volume-confirmed" is real, but "BUY" — the actionable output — inherits none of it. Likewise SELL_SCORE=35 ⇔ raw ≈ 47.

### Signal mapping (signals.ts:536-545 light; 821-828 snapshot)

- **BUY**: compressed ≥ 62 AND trendShare ≥ 0.55 AND (RSI absent or ≤ 78).
- **SELL**: compressed < 35 AND trendShare ≤ 0.45 AND (RSI < 45, or MACD<0 when RSI absent).
- **Everything else**: hold.

Observations:

- The trend-share requirement (0.55) is weak insurance: with EMA50 missing (35-49 sessions) trendMax=15, and price>EMA20 alone = 8/15 = 0.533 — just under the bar; add the slope check and any EMA50 fact and it clears easily. In a full window, price>EMA20 + EMA20>EMA50 = 16/30 = 0.533; any one more trend fact → ≥0.55. So BUY needs only a *plurality* of bullish trend facts, not a stack.
- The RSI≤78 veto is nearly inert: `rsiBandPoints` already gives RSI>78 only 2/10, so a tape with RSI 79 loses at most ~2.4 raw-weighted points — usually still above raw 71. The veto changes almost no decisions; it looks like risk control but is decorative.
- **No zone/level awareness in the mapping at all.** BUY is decided without knowing whether price is 1% under a 4-touch resistance cluster. `at_resistance` is computed for *display* (analyze.ts:242-249) but never feeds the stance. Section C/D examples quantify this.
- SELL requires a *low* score AND bearish trend share AND bearish momentum — three conjunctions. EGX tapes that drift down on thin volume score ~40-55 (extension/volatility terms can carry points) → permanent hold. The engine is structurally much more willing to say BUY than SELL (see distribution in E).

### Correlated-evidence double counting (quantified)

A canonical "plain uptrend, not yet extended" day scores: trend 30/30 (35.0 weighted), momentum ~24-30/30 (RSI 45-60: 10, MACD>0: 10, rangePos 45-75: 10 → 30), volume 14/20 typical (OBV 8 + relVol 1-2× → 6 → 14), risk 15/15 (ext ≤4%, vol ≤3%) → raw ≈ 94 → gate: allDimsStrong? volume 14/20 = 70% → false → clamp 80 → displayed 73 → **BUY**. The same facts (price above rising averages, RSI mid-band, MACD positive) are counted inside trend *and* momentum *and* (inversely) risk. The score is effectively a monotone function of "how uptrendy is the tape," softened at extremes. That is why "signals are unconvincing": a BUY arrives precisely when the tape *looks* best on screen — i.e. after the move — and the four "independent dimensions" do not provide independent confirmation.

### Can strong momentum mask bad risk?

Partially, yes. Risk maxes at 15% weight. Worst risk (ext>8%, daily vol>5%) costs 15 raw points; the same tape gains up to 65 from trend+momentum+volume. A +20%-in-a-month EGX climber (limit-up runs are common on EGX) has trend ~30/30, RSI ~75 (2/10), MACD 10, rangePos ~100 (2/10), volume climax 4-10 → raw ≈ 35+ (14×0.3=… compute: trend 35 + momentum (2+10+2)/30×30 = 14 + volume say 11/20 → 11 + risk 0) ≈ 60 → hold at 53 displayed. So the *most dangerous chase setups* land in hold, not sell — fine — but a milder extension (5-8% above EMA20, still hot RSI 65-72) scores raw ≈ 78-80 → **BUY at 73** while all risk signals scream. The engine's own pinned test blesses a BUY at RSI ≈ 73.6 with rangePos 100 (signals.test.ts:62-87, "buy read at score 67"). Momentum masking risk is not a hypothetical; it is the calibrated happy path.

### Is the 80%-of-max rule meaningful?

As a *display* gate: somewhat (it makes ≥86 rare). As *decision* logic: no — it never binds at the BUY threshold (raw 71.1 < 80). And `allDimsStrong` uses only present dims, so a snapshot missing EMA200 (trendMax 23) needs 18.4/23 — easier than the full-window 24/30 — meaning **shorter-history companies pass the excellence gate more easily than complete ones**, the opposite of the intended conservatism.

### Trade plan (`trade-plan.ts`) — geometry and honesty

- **hold → null plan** (L116): no fake plans for WAIT. Good.
- **Buy, not chasing** (price ≤ 6% above nearest support): entry [max(sup×1.002, lc×0.985), lc×1.004]; stop = sup×(1 − max(0.02, atrPct×1.2/100)) → support-based stop *with* ATR buffer when ATR is known (tab path); stop distance then **clamped into [2%, 8%]** of entry mid (L182-192) — the clamp can silently detach the stop from the very support it claims to be based on (basis text still says "فوق الدعم الأقرب").
- **Buy, chasing** (support >6% away or none): entry [lc×0.985, lc×1.004], stop = **lc×0.955 (flat −4.5%)** unless atrPct>4 → 2×ATR×1.2 (L166-171). On the board path atrPct is always null (signals.ts:593) → every chasing board plan has the same arbitrary −4.5% stop, unrelated to volatility or structure. The plan text still claims ATR/structure anchoring ("مثبتة على أقرب دعم/مقاومة وتقلب ATR", trade-plan.ts:6).
- **Targets**: if the nearest resistance pays ≥1R → aim at it (r1×0.998..×1.004); else **mechanical 2R** = entryMid + 2×stopDist (L196-205). The 2R target is a pure multiplier — it can sit far above the actual resistance wall, or in the middle of nowhere; there is no check that a real zone exists at the target.
- **Sell plan**: target = nearest support **or `lc×0.93` fallback** (L134) — an arbitrary −7% level when no support exists; stop = min(r1×1.01, lc×1.04) — capped at +4%, again arbitrary when the resistance is far.
- **RR is computed, never validated against anything**: by construction RR ≥ 1 (a <1R resistance falls through to the 2R branch), so *every* plan prints a respectable RR — the metric cannot fail, therefore it carries no information. There is no check that the target is reachable (e.g., not beyond a bigger cluster), no spread/liquidity check (EGX spreads on mid-caps can exceed the 0.4% entry band), and **no tick-size handling anywhere in the repo** (grep: no tick logic; all levels rounded to 0.01 by `roundPrice`). EGX price bands/tick conventions are simply ignored.
- **Stale-support risk**: the entry zone uses the *nearest pivot cluster below the close*, which after a long run is often a stale minor shelf 6%+ below → the "chasing" branch fires → arbitrary percentages. I.e., **the more extended the tape, the less structural the plan** — inverted from what prudence requires.

### StyleFits (`computeStyleFits`, signals.ts:643-708)

Four checklists filtered to `earned ≥ ceil(total/2)`:

- **day**: requires only relVol≥1.3, |Δ|≥1.5%, dailyVol≥1.5%, and signal ∈ {buy, hold} — note a **hold** day with a hot move qualifies as a "day trade" (the 4th check credits hold!). No liquidity/spread floor (relVol is a ratio, not absolute turnover), no session-timing check — on EGX many "1.5%+ moves" happen on limit-locked illiquid names where a day trade cannot actually be executed.
- **swing**: price>EMA20, EMA20>EMA50, RSI 40-75, rangePos 25-85, MACD hist>0, and signal=buy **or hold-with-rising-EMA20** — again hold qualifies with 5/6 from pure trend facts.
- **trend**: price>EMA50, slope, MACD, OBV, and signal=buy (1/5 needs the actual buy).
- **reversal**: gated on RSI<40; needs RSI≤38, an up close, MACD hist>0, relVol≥1.

styleFits are derived from the *same* correlated facts as the score (trend/momentum blocks dominate swing/trend), so they mostly re-print the stance at other horizons; they add execution-horizon flavor, not independent evidence. The snapshot path hard-codes `rangePos60: null` and `ema20SlopeUp: null` (signals.ts:905-907) — so on the company tab the swing checklist can never earn its range-position point and the trend checklist never its slope point: **the same company shows different style fits on board vs tab** even after the canonical-stance overlay (the overlay copies the tab's fits to the board, so the *board* loses those points too whenever the canonical stance exists).

## C) Structure / quality layers — computed, displayed, and (mostly) dead

- **structure.ts (structure@1)** — solid deterministic implementation: strict fractal pivots (wing=3), same-kind collapse, 1.5% noise filter, HH/LH/HL/LL labels, trend from last two highs+lows, BOS/CHoCH with volume ratio + retest + invalidation, weekly resample for the long timeframe (R1-R9, all tested in intelligence.test.ts:78-185). Point-in-time discipline is explicitly handled (events computed from swings *before* the pivot). **But the stance never reads it.** `computeStanceFromSnapshot` accesses `snap.trend/momentum/volume/volatility/levels` only. `snap.structure` flows into the payload and (per comments) the AI narrative — the human sees a beautiful BOS/CHoCH map that has zero influence on whether the score says BUY. A bearish CHoCH on the day of a BUY is displayed right next to the BUY, contradicting it, with no reconciliation (the only conflict-detection that existed, `critical_review` in quality.ts:175-210, is itself removed from the bundle).
- **zones.ts (zones@1)** — F1 consolidation-break + F2 pivot-rejection zones with quality scores, retest counts, invalidation. Two real defects: (1) **F2 reaction is a placeholder**: `pivotRejectionZones` sets `reactionPct = |cluster price − lastClose|/lastClose` with the comment "provisional; replaced by real reaction below" (zones.ts:197) — nothing ever replaces it, so the zone *quality* score (reaction = min(20, reactionPct×4), zones.ts:207-214) is actually scoring *distance from the current price*, i.e. far-from-price zones score higher "reaction" — inverted semantics; (2) F1's retest counter walks forward from the break and stops at the first 1.5% adverse close (L142-150), under-counting later retests. Zones are computed into `snap.zones` (analyze.ts:581) and rendered, **never used by the scorer or the trade plan** (trade-plan uses `levels` = `supportResistance()`, a different, simpler algorithm — the tab shows two different S/R systems: "levels" driving stops, "zones" displayed beside them).
- **vsa.ts (vsa@1)** — careful, guardrailed VSA events (climax/no-demand/effort-no-result with follow-through). **Hard-removed**: `base.vsa = null` since technical@4 (analyze.ts:578, "removed per owner directive"). ~220 lines of dead production code.
- **quality.ts (quality@1)** — 9-dimension analytical-completeness score with a gate veto. **Also hard-removed** (`base.quality = null`, analyze.ts:588). Its most valuable idea — flagging conflicts (trend up but RSI weak; structure bearish while trend bullish; timeframes disagree) — is exactly what the product lacks, and it is switched off.
- **What actually feeds the decision**: EMA facts, RSI, MACD, (Stoch on tab), OBV slope, relVol, extension, daily-vol/ATR, and pivot-S/R levels only for invalidation/plan anchoring. Everything else in the bundle is narrative decoration.

## D) Snapshot / versioning / staleness

- **Version stack**: `technical@4` (bundle shape, analyze.ts:38), `tech-signals@4` (scoring weights/bands, signals.ts:35), `structure@1`, `zones@1`, `stance@1` (stance store), board cache key `technical-signals:market:v3`, board read model `technical-signals:board:v1`. Version bumps are honored at read time (engineVersion mismatch → recompute/reject, stance-store.ts:108, signals-service.ts:210). **Documentation drift**: the signals.ts header block (L123-145) still describes @3 calibration (gate 85, relVol 1.2, exponent 1.25) while the code implements @4 (gate 80, relVol 1.3, exponent 1.4) — a future recalibration reading the header would mis-derive the anchors; the inline step-3 comment (L169-171) is correct (@4). The @3 examples in that comment also contain a rounding slip ("80 → 74" — actual 80^1.4 → 73).
- **Canonical stance store (stance@1)**: one row per company (unique companyId), upserted on compute. `getCanonicalStance` serves the persisted JSON while `computedAt` < **20h** (`STALE_AFTER_MS`), else recomputes from the full bundle and persists (stance-store.ts:120-152). Board overlay trusts persisted stances up to **7 days** (`BOARD_MAX_AGE_MS`) with matching engineVersion (signals-service.ts:210). A stance-refresh cron recomputes the 40 stales companies per run, missing-first/stales-first (stance-store.ts:158-210) — with ~246 active companies, a full refresh cycle needs ≥7 cron runs; on a daily cron the oldest stances are ~6-7 days old, i.e. **the board can legally show a week-old reading as the "canonical" stance** (dataAsOf travels, but the UI treatment of that age is outside this audit).
- **Board snapshot**: whole-market compute cached 10 min; cold instances serve the persisted Setting snapshot if ≤15 min (market hours) / ≤24h (off hours) old (board-snapshot.ts:17-18, 33-35). Market-hours check `hour < 14.5` on an integer hour is a no-op quirk (Cairo hour is integral, so this is `hour ≤ 14`, closing at 14:59 not 14:30 — harmless but sloppy).
- **Staleness semantics on data**: the tab bundle merges PriceBar (frozen fallback) + Yahoo 2y daily (15-min in-memory cache; best-effort, **datacenter-IP rate-limited — this sandbox reproduces exactly that with 429s**) + TradingView scanner close-only day-rows (freshest, O=H=L=C). Corporate-action adjustment rescales the series before indicators (analyze.ts:390-413) — a real and well-documented fix (CANA −31.9% incident). Limitations are disclosed honestly. The board runs on 300 calendar days (≈200 sessions) of close-only rows — **EMA200/golden-cross facts are structurally absent from board scoring**, and after corporate-action rescaling the board and tab can even disagree on adjusted history depth (tab: up to 400 bars incl. Yahoo; board: 300 days, no Yahoo).
- **What happens when snapshots are stale**: board → full recompute (or 24h-old snapshot off-hours); tab → recompute on access after 20h; stance cron → recompute 40/run. A stale-but-within-7d stance is *not* recomputed when data changes intraday — a BUY issued Monday can still be shown Friday with Friday's price alongside it, only the small `dataAsOf` field betraying the age.

### E.2 What the numbers prove, ranked

1. **The BUY decision ignores its own evidence requirements.** Over 3,465 real BUY days, the majority (53.9%) were issued with price in the top 20% of its 60-session range, and 62.6% on below-average signal-day volume — 29.6% at less than *half* average volume. The mechanism check shows why: the "excellence gate" (`s > 80` → clamp, signals.ts:163-171) sits **above** the BUY threshold (compressed 62 ⇔ raw 71), so any raw 71-80 tape prints a BUY regardless of participation. The relVol-band design (signals.ts:239-245) and the RSI≤78 veto lose at most ~4 weighted points — never enough to push raw below 71. This is the single biggest driver of "unconvincing" reads: the label fires precisely when the chart *looks* most extended, and volume confirmation is optional.
2. **The label carries ~no short-horizon information.** 5-session forward returns after BUY (median 0.0%, IQR [−2.5, +3.2]) are statistically indistinguishable from after HOLD (0.0%, [−2.0, +2.5]) — and *worse than after SELL* (+0.6%). 49.3% of BUY days were followed by a negative 5-session return. The four "independent dimensions" (section B: one phenomenon counted three times) do not create independent confirmation, and the reader gets no edge from the label at the horizon the style tags advertise ("صفقة أيام").
3. **The board is bipolar: whipsaw for trended names, dead-band for the rest.** Median signal run = 3 sessions with ~100 transitions per ticker over 2y (COMI BUY→HOLD→BUY within 4 sessions, twice), while ORAS printed **hold for 467 consecutive sessions** (score pinned 27-45: too bearish-trending to buy, never "sell" because the three-way SELL conjunction — score <35 AND trendShare ≤0.45 AND bearish momentum — is too strict). Meanwhile 48.3% of all scored days sit in the 55-75 score band — one slow-average crossing away from flipping — and the 1.4 compression curve then amplifies those band crossings into displayed jumps of −61 (OCDI 2025-08-17), −56 (RAYA 2024-12-22), −54 (HRHO 2024-12-10) in a single session. A product whose labels flip every 3 days and whose scores jump ±50 overnight cannot feel trustworthy.
4. **Large single-session information is smoothed away.** On 15.8% of ≥3% sessions the score moved ≤1 point — COMI's −7.84% session (2025-12-08) left the score unchanged. Trend/momentum are slow windows; risk maxes at 15% weight; so crash days do not move the read while slow EMA crossings jump it. The score's sensitivity profile is inverted relative to risk.
5. **Trade plans are mostly mechanical arithmetic dressed as structure.** Median 74.9% of BUY plans print RR = exactly 2.00 (the 2R rule — no level), the modal stop is the flat −4.5% chasing stop (`lc×0.955`, trade-plan.ts:170) with no ATR on the board path, and 99.1% of SELL days produce a plan (often with the arbitrary `lc×0.93` target fallback, trade-plan.ts:134). In live production the picture is worse: `canonicalCount=5` on the board means **trade plans are nulled for 239 of 244 rows** (signals-service.ts:232-237) — the flagship "deterministic plan" feature is visible for 2% of the board.
6. **The public data path starves the engine.** The only public history endpoint serves ~16 sparse close points (no volume) for every ticker; the board's own 300-day window lacks EMA200 facts (every free-ticker production stance shows trend max 23, not 30); a listed company (NAPR) has zero coverage end-to-end; and Yahoo ghost bars on EGX holidays (EAST 2024-12-25, 2025-07-27) pass the engine's bar hygiene and act as real sessions with relVol 0.03-0.04. Thin and dirty inputs cap how "convincing" any scoring layer above them can be.

### E.4 Synthesis for the product owner

The engine's *math* is clean (Wilder/EMA/MACD/ATR implementations are correct and test-pinned) and its *honesty scaffolding* (null-with-reason, caps, disclosures) is genuinely good. The unconvincing output comes from decision-layer design, in order: (1) the BUY threshold sits below the volume/excellence gate so confirmation is optional — and 62.6% of BUYs indeed lack it; (2) trend+momentum+risk score one phenomenon three times, so the score tracks "how extended the uptrend looks" — exactly when entry is worst; (3) the SELL side is nearly unreachable (ORAS: two years of hold) and the label flips every ~3 sessions in trended names; (4) levels/zones/structure/VSA exist but never arbitrate the decision (47.7% of BUYs under resistance, CHoCHs printed next to BUYs); (5) the plans are mechanical percentages (RR 2.00 everywhere, −4.5% stops) and are actually shown for only 5 of 244 live board rows. A minimal high-impact fix list — none of it implemented here: make relVol ≥ ~1.0 (or OBV agreement) a hard BUY precondition; demote the triple-counted trend facts into one trend *state* input with a single weight; extend the excellence gate down to the BUY threshold (gate the decision, not the display); wire `levels`/`zones` into the mapping (veto BUY within ~2% of a multi-touch resistance); widen the SELL band (drop the trendShare conjunction, or add a distribution/CHoCH path); and cap score display drift (smooth or hysteresis the label, disclose changes) so reads persist days, not hours.
